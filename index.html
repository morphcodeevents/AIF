01 / Context & Opportunity 02
The Communication Challenge

HIV has changed. Public perception hasn't caught up.

The gap between medical progress and persistent social stigma presents an opportunity to create more informed public conversations around HIV.

Medical Progress (The Reality)
Treatment makes HIV uninfectious & manageable
  • ✔ U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable): Effective antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses viral load to undetectable levels, completely preventing sexual transmission.
  • ✔ Modern Life Expectancy: People living with HIV on sustained treatment live full, healthy, and productive lives.
  • ✔ Zero Casual Transmission: HIV cannot be transmitted through saliva, sharing food, utensils, drinking glasses, or shaking hands.
Social Stigma (The Perception Gap)
Outdated fears continue to dictate public behaviour
  • ✕ Everyday Avoidance: Lingering irrational anxiety around sharing glasses, sharing meals, and physical proximity in daily social life.
  • ✕ Reluctance to Test: Moral judgment and fear of isolation prevent at-risk and general populations from early, routine screening.
  • ✕ Silence in Relationships: Status disclosure remains paralyzed by fear of rejection, workplace prejudice, and legal discrimination.
Strategic Imperative: Close the divide between biomedical advancement and everyday societal acceptance. Actionable Territory
Strategic Rationale: Perception Lag Analysis

02 / Creative Direction 03
Parent Creative Proposition

THE THINGS
WE DON'T SAY

“A conversation we need to have.”

Stigma thrives in unspoken hesitation — the split-second pause before sharing a drink, the awkward silence when HIV is mentioned, the assumption that it only happens to “other people.”

The Things We Don't Say surfaces these unspoken assumptions directly in public spaces. It does not lecture or shame; it creates safe, candid, provocative moments of truth that dismantle prejudice through informed dialogue.

04 / Pillar One: Street Activations 05
Activation Deliverable · 15 Edited Assets

15 edited VoxPop videos across three creative formats

Filmed across high-energy Bengaluru hubs (Church Street, Indiranagar, Koramangala, college campuses) with full participant informed consent.

Format 01 · Social Experiment

The Glass

Using a glass or water bottle as a physical conversation starter in public spaces to challenge deep-rooted misconceptions around transmission through shared food, drinks, and utensils.

Street Interaction Prompt
“Would you take a sip from this glass if you knew the person before you was HIV positive?”
Format 02 · Behavioral Test

Everyday Interactions

Exploring whether knowing someone's HIV status alters ordinary, everyday social interactions — sharing a taxi, shaking hands, sharing an office desk, or borrowing clothes.

Street Interaction Prompt
“Your colleague discloses their HIV status at lunch. Does anything change between you?”
Format 03 · Scenario Dilemma

Would You Say Yes?

Presenting young Bengaluru urbanites with hypothetical real-life dilemmas spanning romantic relationships, marriage, employment, renting an apartment, and healthcare access.

Street Interaction Prompt
“The person you’re dating is on treatment and U=U (undetectable). Would you marry them?”

06 / Pillar Three: Public Insight 07
Digital Survey & Advocacy Bridge

Bengaluru HIV Awareness & Perception Pulse 2026

A lightweight, anonymous digital assessment generating actionable evidence on urban awareness, stigma patterns, and knowledge of rights.

Survey Capture Dimensions

  • HIV Awareness & Transmission Myths: Baseline knowledge of transmission vs social contact misconceptions.
  • Understanding of U=U: Public familiarity with viral suppression and sexual untransmittability.
  • Social Attitudes & Stigma Index: Comfort levels with colleagues, flatmates, and partners living with HIV.
  • Rights & Legal Literacy: Awareness of protections under the HIV & AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017.
  • Demographic Correlates: Broad age bracket (18–25, 26–35, 36+) and Bengaluru quadrant clusters.
Rigorous Privacy Guarantee

Strictly anonymous. The survey will never collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, IP identifiers, or respondents' personal HIV status.

Methodological Qualification

Findings will be communicated as a Campaign Pulse (capturing engaged urban respondents), rather than a representative citywide study, maintaining scientific integrity.

Advocacy Contribution

Consolidated findings will be packaged into a polished executive perception report for AHF India Cares to support policy, stakeholder, and community advocacy.

Ethics: Non-invasive · Zero PII Collection · Voluntary Participation
